I own a laptop. I am not a Luddite making a point. I write these essays in it, eventually, and I would not attempt a novel-length draft any other way. But the first pass of nearly everything I write still happens in fountain pen ink, in a notebook that costs more per unit than it has any business costing, for reasons I have spent a long time trying to justify to people who, understandably, think this is an affectation.

My best guess at the reason: a fountain pen cannot keep up with a first draft, and I have come to rely on that.

A keyboard can move as fast as you can think, which sounds like an advantage until you read what it produces: a draft that keeps every half-formed detour your mind takes before it lands on the sentence it meant to write. A pen forces a small delay between the thought and the mark on the page, just long enough for the sentence to get slightly better before it commits to paper. You cannot easily delete on paper. Deletion costs a strikethrough, visible and a little embarrassing, so you learn, slowly, to want the sentence right the first time rather than fixing it the fourth.

There is also the matter of pressure. A fountain pen, unlike a ballpoint, responds to how hard you push. Line width shifts subtly with the weight of your hand, which means your handwriting on an anxious day looks physically different from your handwriting on a calm one. I did not expect a writing instrument to be a mood diary, but mine apparently is.

None of this is an argument that you should buy one. Pens leak. Cartridges run dry at the worst moment. A good one costs more than several word processors' worth of software, and none of that expense makes a bad sentence into a good one. The tool has never been the thing that makes writing work; _attention_ is the thing that makes writing work, and a fountain pen is just one of the stranger ways I have found to enforce a little more of it on myself. If a cheap gel pen or a blank document does the same for you, you have lost nothing by skipping mine.

What I would say, cautiously, to anyone who drafts entirely on a keyboard and has never tried otherwise: the friction is not a bug you have been correctly optimizing away. For a first draft, at least, it might have been doing some of the actual work.
